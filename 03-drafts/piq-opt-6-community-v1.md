# Optional PIQ #7 Community - v1
Prompt: What have you done to make your school or your community a better place?

Most afternoons, the 3D printers at the FLC Innovation Center sat idle. I'd walk past around 2 p.m. and count the people inside. Two. Sometimes zero. Down the hall, the tutoring center was full of engineering majors working problem sets.

Folsom Lake is a commuter college. Most STEM students I knew drove in for class, went to work, and drove home. They could solve a circuit on paper but had never wired one. I learned more from my first breadboard that didn't work than from a week of lecture, and I wanted other students to get that chance without figuring it out alone.

As Workshops Coordinator for the FLC Math & Engineering Club, I set up biweekly hands-on sessions in Aspen Hall, room FL1-130. I rotated three topics: Fusion 360, which is 3D design software; Arduino breadboarding, which means building circuits on a reusable board without soldering; and Python for physics labs, so students could stop plotting lab data by hand.

The first Arduino night went badly. I had planned too much, and half the room ran out of time before a single LED blinked. I cut the next session down to one circuit and spent the extra time walking table to table, checking wiring with people instead of for them. More of them stayed until it worked.

When the FLC Tech Summit came up, I handled logistics for 3 student teams so they could focus on their builds. Some of those students had first touched a microcontroller in our room.

I took the same approach off campus. At Folsom Public Library, I help run biweekly youth STEM sessions for 10-15 middle schoolers, debugging Scratch and Python projects and building paper rockets and popsicle-stick bridges. During the 8-week robotics build season, I mentor Folsom-area FRC and VEX high school teams on drivetrain assembly, CAD tolerances, and wiring cleanup before regional qualifiers.

A lab is only useful if people feel allowed to walk in. At UC, I want to keep opening that door for the next student who has only seen circuits on paper.
