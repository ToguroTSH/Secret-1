# Optional PIQ #4: Educational Opportunity or Barrier - v3
Prompt: Describe how you have taken advantage of a significant educational opportunity or worked to overcome an educational barrier you have faced.

At Folsom Lake College's pace, my engineering prerequisites would have taken three to four years. Math and physics ran only a few sections, and labs had waitlists. Open sections clashed with each other or with my 12 hours a week at Jack in the Box. If I missed one seat, every course behind it would slip a full year.

So I built the schedule myself. FLC Transfer Center workshops showed me how ASSIST articulation works, and I mapped every electrical engineering prerequisite on it. When FLC had no seat that fit my week, I found a transferable section at another community college. Before registering, I logged each course, college, term, and articulation match in one spreadsheet.

The math was the hardest piece. Physics 411 and ENGR 420 depend on multivariable calculus and differential equations, and FLC's sections would not fit one summer. Coastline offered Calc 3 and a combined Differential Equations and Linear Algebra course. Before enrolling, I checked ASSIST campus by campus to confirm one course covered both requirements everywhere I was applying. I took both that summer.

That plan took me to six colleges, each for a seat or time slot FLC could not give me. In Summer 2026 I was enrolled at four of them at once, taking eight courses. Some nights I had four course portals open and a shift the next day. I doubted I could keep my grades up.

This fall I am taking Physics 411, Chem 400, CISP 400, and ENGR 420 at FLC, with classmates carrying loads like mine. I organized the FLC STEM Study Group, and six to eight of us meet three hours every week to work problem sets on the whiteboard. The condensed formula sheet I bring each week is also how I study for my own exams.

My grades held. I have a 3.97 GPA and made the President's and Dean's Honor List every semester. I am on track to complete about 74 transferable units by Spring 2027. When I started Physics 411 this fall, the math it runs on was already finished.
