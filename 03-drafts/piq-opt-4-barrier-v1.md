# Optional PIQ #4: Educational Opportunity or Barrier - v1
Prompt: Describe how you have taken advantage of a significant educational opportunity or worked to overcome an educational barrier you have faced.

At Folsom Lake College's pace, my engineering prerequisites would have taken three to four years. Math and physics ran in a few sections, labs filled with waitlists, and open times clashed with each other or with my 12 hours a week at Jack in the Box. Missing one seat would push every course behind it back a full year.

So I built the schedule myself. I opened ASSIST, the state database that shows which community college courses count at each UC, and mapped my requirements one course at a time. Then I searched other community colleges for sections that fit my week and would transfer. I kept a spreadsheet of every course, college, term, and articulation match, and checked each line twice before registering.

The plan put me at six colleges. In Spring 2026 I took English at Foothill and Ethnic Studies at Palomar. That summer I stacked Calc 3 and Differential Equations/Linear Algebra at Coastline, economics at Sierra, and statistics, biology, and communication at Long Beach City. Clearing the math in summer meant I started Physics 411, Chem 400, and ENGR 420 this fall ready for the equations instead of catching up on them.

It was a lot. Some nights I logged into three different course portals and still had a shift the next day. I doubted I could keep my grades up.

Over breaks I worked through MIT OpenCourseWare modules in aerodynamics, propulsion, and systems engineering. I also taught myself Laplace transforms and transfer functions, the math that predicts whether a control system settles or swings out of control.

The result is about 74 transferable units and a 3.97 GPA. This spring I'll take Physics 421, Physics 431, and ENGR 400. Classmates started asking how I was keeping up, so I turned my system into the FLC STEM Study Group: weekly three-hour sessions for 6-8 peers in Calc 3, Linear Algebra, Physics 411, and Chem 400, with condensed formula sheets and whiteboard problem sets.

No one handed me this route. I learned to read requirements closely enough to find one, and now other students use it too.
