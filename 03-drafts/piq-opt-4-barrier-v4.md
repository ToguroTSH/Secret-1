# Optional PIQ #4: Educational Opportunity or Barrier - v4 (voice pass)
Prompt: Describe how you have taken advantage of a significant educational opportunity or worked to overcome an educational barrier you have faced.

At Folsom Lake College's pace, my engineering prerequisites were going to take three to four years. Math and physics only ran a few sections, labs had waitlists, and the open sections clashed with each other or with my 12 hours a week at Jack in the Box. Missing one seat meant every course behind it slipped a full year.

So I built my own schedule. The FLC Transfer Center workshops showed me how ASSIST articulation works, and I mapped every electrical engineering prerequisite on it myself. When FLC had no seat that fit my week, I found a transferable section at another community college and logged it in one spreadsheet, with the course, college, term, and articulation match, before I registered.

I wanted the math finished before Physics 411 and Statics. FLC's sections wouldn't fit into one summer, but Coastline offered Calc 3 and a combined Differential Equations and Linear Algebra course. Before I enrolled, I checked ASSIST campus by campus to make sure that one course covered both requirements everywhere I was applying. I took both that summer.

That plan took me to six colleges, each one for a seat or a time slot FLC couldn't give me. In Summer 2026 I was enrolled at four of them at once, taking eight courses. Some nights I had four course portals open and a shift the next day. I honestly wasn't sure I could keep my grades up.

This fall I'm taking Physics 411, Chem 400, CISP 400, and Statics at FLC, alongside classmates carrying loads like mine. I organized the FLC STEM Study Group, where six to eight of us meet for three hours every week and work problem sets on the whiteboard. The condensed formula sheet I bring each week ends up being how I study for my own exams too.

My grades held. I have a 3.97 GPA, I've made the President's and Dean's Honor List every semester, and I'm on track to complete about 74 transferable units by Spring 2027. When Physics 411 started this fall, the math was already done.
