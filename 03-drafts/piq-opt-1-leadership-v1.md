# Optional PIQ #1 Leadership - v1
Prompt: Describe an example of your leadership experience in which you have positively influenced others, helped resolve disputes or contributed to group efforts over time.

"Where's my food? I've been standing here fifteen minutes."

Friday dinner rush at Jack in the Box. The drive-thru line wrapped past the menu board, my headset kept beeping, and a customer was leaning over the front counter. Our newest hire stood frozen at the register, her finger over the wrong button. I had trained her on that screen two weeks earlier.

I stepped beside her, voided the bad entry, and said, "Combo first, then the drink. I'll take the next one." She finished the order. The line kept growing.

The register was the small problem. Every time ticket times spiked, the front counter and the kitchen turned on each other. Cashiers said the cooks were slow. Cooks said the cashiers sent orders in wrong. One night a cook yelled, "Fix your screen before you send me garbage." Nobody fixed anything. We just got slower.

As Shift Trainer for 4 new hires, I noticed they were learning the blame faster than the menu, so I changed my training. I taught the register as the first step of the kitchen's work and had each hire watch their own tickets come up on the line to see what a missed modifier did to a cook. Before each peak, I staged cups and sauces so nobody scrambled. I started short callouts between stations: "Big order coming, six combos." When a bottleneck hit, I moved to the worst spot and kept my voice level.

It did not work right away. For weeks, one cook ignored the callouts and the arguments continued. I was tempted to cover every station myself. Instead I asked him what was breaking his station. "Everything hits me at once," he said. So we agreed the counter would call large orders before ringing them in.

I kept that routine while working 12 hours a week next to a full STEM course load. By spring, the new hires made callouts without prompting, and rushes got quieter. I learned that leading a team under pressure mostly happens before the pressure starts, in how people are trained to see each other's work.
