# Optional PIQ #4: Educational Opportunity or Barrier - FINAL
Prompt: Describe how you have taken advantage of a significant educational opportunity or worked to overcome an educational barrier you have faced.

Three to four years. That's how long my engineering prerequisites would have taken at Folsom Lake College's pace. Math and physics ran only a few sections, labs had waitlists, and the open sections clashed with each other or with my 12 hours a week at Jack in the Box. Miss one seat, and every course behind it slipped a full year.

So I built my own schedule. FLC's Transfer Center workshops showed me how ASSIST articulation works, and I mapped every electrical engineering prerequisite on it myself. When FLC didn't have a seat that fit my week, I found a transferable section at another community college and logged it in one spreadsheet: course, college, term, articulation match. All before I registered.

I wanted the math done before Physics 411 and Statics. FLC's sections wouldn't fit into one summer. Coastline offered Calc 3 and a combined Differential Equations and Linear Algebra course, but I needed to know one course would count as two. I checked ASSIST campus by campus until I knew it covered both requirements everywhere I was applying. Then I took both that summer.

That plan took me to six colleges. Each one gave me a seat or a time slot FLC couldn't. In Summer 2026, I was enrolled at four of them at once, taking eight courses. Some nights I had four course portals open and a shift the next day. Honestly, I wasn't sure my grades would survive it.

This fall I'm at FLC for Physics 411, Chem 400, CISP 400, and Statics. A lot of my classmates carry loads like mine, so I organized the FLC STEM Study Group. Six to eight of us meet three hours every week and work problem sets on the whiteboard. The condensed formula sheet I bring each week turned into how I study for my own exams.

My grades held: a 3.97 GPA and the President's and Dean's Honor List every semester. I'm on track to complete about 74 transferable units by Spring 2027. When Physics 411 started this fall, the math was already done.
