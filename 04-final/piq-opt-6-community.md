# Optional PIQ #6 Community - FINAL
Prompt: What have you done to make your school or your community a better place?

Most afternoons, the Innovation Center makerspace at Folsom Lake College sat nearly empty, with idle 3D printers and clear benches. FLC is a commuter college, and most STEM students I knew drove in for class, went to work, and drove home. They could solve a circuit on paper but had never wired one, and few knew the makerspace was open to them.

I knew what they were missing because my own Tech Summit team had spent hours chasing a power-draw problem on our solar tracker. As Workshops Coordinator for the FLC Math & Engineering Club, I ran hands-on sessions in that room, Aspen Hall FL1-130, every other week, rotating Fusion 360, Arduino breadboarding, and Python for physics labs. A typical session drew 12 to 18 students, many building hardware for the first time.

My first Arduino night went badly. I planned too much, and most of the room ran out of time before an LED blinked, so I cut the next session to one circuit and let students find their own mistakes.

A few weeks before the Summit, a first-time builder was wiring a light-tracking rig with a photoresistor and a micro servo. Their sensor readings looked clean, but the servo twitched and locked up every time they uploaded code. They were about to delete it and start over. Instead of taking the breadboard, I sat down with a multimeter and had them trace where the servo's current returned. They had powered the servo from a 4-AA pack so it would not brown out the Arduino, but never tied the pack's ground to the Arduino's. I explained that voltage only means something relative to a shared zero, and they ran one jumper from the battery's negative rail to GND. The servo started following their phone flashlight. "Wait, that's it? One wire?" They stayed 30 minutes late tuning thresholds and signed up for a Tech Summit team that night.

Three club teams entered the Summit. I booked their bench time and tracked down missing parts, and all three built in the makerspace, at benches that used to sit empty.
