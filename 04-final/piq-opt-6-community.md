# Optional PIQ #6 Community - FINAL
Prompt: What have you done to make your school or your community a better place?

Most afternoons, the FLC Innovation Center makerspace sat nearly empty. The 3D printers were idle, and the benches were clear.

Folsom Lake is a commuter college. Most STEM students I knew drove in for class, went to work, and drove home. They could solve a circuit on paper but had never wired one, and few knew the makerspace was open to them.

On my own Tech Summit team, the four of us wired photoresistors, servos, and a microcontroller into a solar-tracking panel, then chased a power-draw problem for hours before it worked well enough to reach the hardware finals. Few of my classmates had ever spent an afternoon like that at a bench.

As Workshops Coordinator for the FLC Math & Engineering Club, I ran our hands-on sessions in that same room, Aspen Hall FL1-130, every other week for 28 weeks. I rotated Fusion 360 basics, Arduino breadboarding, and Python for physics labs. A typical session drew 12 to 18 students, many building hardware for the first time.

The first Arduino night went badly. I had planned too much, and most of the room ran out of time before a single LED blinked. For the next session I cut the plan to one circuit. When a board stayed dark, I pointed to the row to check and let the student find the wrong jumper. More students stayed until their LED lit.

Off campus, I assist library staff with biweekly STEM sessions for 10 to 15 middle schoolers at Folsom Public Library, where I help kids debug their first Scratch and Python projects.

When three club teams entered the FLC Tech Summit, I handled their logistics. Beforehand, I booked makerspace bench time for their builds and tracked down the parts each team was missing. On demo day I set up their tables and power. Some of their members had wired their first circuit at one of our Arduino nights, and at the Summit those first-time builders demoed working breadboard prototypes to faculty judges. All three teams had built in the makerspace, at the benches that used to sit empty.
