# Optional PIQ #7 Community - FINAL
Prompt: What have you done to make your school or your community a better place?

Most afternoons, the 3D printers at the FLC Innovation Center sat idle, with no students around them.

Folsom Lake is a commuter college. Most STEM students I knew drove in for class, went to work, and drove home. They could solve a circuit on paper but had never wired one.

I learned what they were missing on my own Tech Summit team. The four of us wired photoresistors, servos, and a microcontroller into a solar-tracking panel. We chased a power-draw problem for hours before it worked well enough to make the hardware finals. Most of my classmates had never spent hours like that at a bench, and nobody had told them the Innovation Center was open to them.

As Workshops Coordinator for the FLC Math & Engineering Club, I organized hands-on sessions every other week across 28 weeks in Aspen Hall, room FL1-130. I rotated three topics: Fusion 360 basics, Arduino breadboarding, and Python for physics labs. The Python sessions were there so students could stop plotting lab data by hand.

The first Arduino night went badly. I had planned too much, and most of the room ran out of time before a single LED blinked. For the next session I cut the plan to one circuit. When a board stayed dark, I pointed to the section of the breadboard to check and let the student find the wrong jumper on their own. More of them stayed until their LED lit.

Off campus, I assist library staff with biweekly STEM sessions for 10-15 middle schoolers at Folsom Public Library. During the 8-week robotics build season, I also help Folsom-area FRC and VEX high school teams with drivetrain assembly and wiring cleanup before regional qualifiers.

For the FLC Tech Summit, I handled logistics for 3 student teams so they could spend their time on their builds. Some of the students on those teams had first touched a microcontroller at one of our workshops. At the Summit, I watched them demo working breadboard prototypes to faculty judges.
