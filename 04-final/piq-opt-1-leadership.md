# Optional PIQ #1 Leadership - FINAL
Prompt: Describe an example of your leadership experience in which you have positively influenced others, helped resolve disputes or contributed to group efforts over time.

"Where's my food?"

Friday dinner rush at Jack in the Box. The drive-thru line wrapped past the menu board and my headset kept beeping. A customer was leaning over the front counter while our newest hire stood frozen at the register, her finger over the wrong button. I had trained her on that screen myself.

I stepped beside her, voided the bad entry, and said, "Combo first, then the drink. I'll take the next one." She finished the order. The line kept growing.

The bigger problem showed up every time ticket times spiked, when the front counter and the kitchen turned on each other. Cashiers said the cooks were slow. Cooks said the cashiers sent orders in wrong. One night a cook yelled, "Fix your screen before you send me garbage." Nobody fixed anything, and we got slower.

As Shift Trainer for 4 new hires, I started hearing them blame the kitchen too, so I changed how I trained them. I still taught POS and food safety, but I also taught that every order rung at the register becomes a cook's ticket. Each hire rang practice orders, then watched their own tickets come up on the line to see what a missed modifier did to a cook. Before each peak, I staged cups and sauces so nobody scrambled. I started short callouts between stations: "Big order coming." When a bottleneck hit, I moved to the worst spot and kept my voice level.

It did not work right away. For weeks, one cook ignored the callouts and the arguments kept going. I wanted to cover every station myself. Instead I asked him what was breaking his station. "Everything hits me at once," he said. We agreed the counter would call large orders before ringing them in. Once he could see a rush coming, he stopped yelling at the register.

Over the following months, the new hires made callouts without prompting. Most of my work as a trainer happened before the rush, in teaching each person what their part of an order does to the person next to them.
